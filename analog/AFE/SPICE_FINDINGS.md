# What the 16-channel netlist told us

`analog/AFE/netlists/afe16_xlse.cir`, generated by `scripts/gen_afe16.py`,
is the first time the diode-OR threshold has been simulated rather than
argued. Run it:

```bash
python3 analog/AFE/scripts/gen_afe16.py --no-tran
cd analog/AFE/netlists && ngspice -b afe16_xlse.cir
```

Drop `--no-tran` for the tone burst (~50 s, writes
`artifacts/afe16_tran.csv`). `--delta <mV>` moves V_ref off the quiescent
point; it is the SPICE twin of `xmax_floor_frac` in training.

Per-channel topology is unchanged from `AFE_tuning/netlists/v15_channel.cir`,
which matches the colleague's board. The only new hardware is the reference.

---

## 1. The OR node really is a log-sum-exp

This was the load-bearing claim of the whole `xlse` design and it had only
ever been derived on paper. Sweeping one channel 80 mV with the other 15 held
at quiescent, and fitting `V_max = LSE_T(v) + offset`:

| I_sink | best-fit n | T = n·V_T | residual RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 nA | 1.045 | 27.03 mV | 93.6 µV |
| **1 µA** | **1.046** | **27.06 mV** | **8.8 µV** |
| 10 µA | 1.044 | 27.00 mV | 10.7 µV |

At 1 µA the residual is 8.8 µV over an 80 mV sweep — 0.011%. The circuit is a
log-sum-exp, not an approximation of one.

Two things fall out:

**T is now measured, not assumed.** 27.06 mV, i.e. n_eff = 1.046. We had been
carrying n somewhere in 1.0–1.5 and quoting the overshoot bound as a
71.7–107.5 mV range. It is the bottom of that range: **T·ln16 = 75.0 mV**.

**I_sink = 1 µA is right for a reason now.** 100 nA is only 45× the diode's
I_s, so the −1 in the diode equation stops being negligible and the fit
degrades 10×. 10 µA buys nothing and costs 10× the power. 1 µA ≈ 1.8 µW.

Note I_sink does *not* set the LSE temperature — that is n·V_T at any current.
It only sets V_d, and the matched diode in the buffer feedback cancels V_d
exactly. The residual −5.0 mV offset is the op-amp, not the diodes (§4).

## 2. Silence margin is entirely the LSE floor

At δ = 0 all 16 comparators are quiet at silence, and the margin is exactly

    margin_c = alpha_c · (T·ln16)   [measured 70.0 mV after the op-amp offset]

| ch | alpha | margin |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.8745 | 61.2 mV |
| 6 | 0.1725 | 12.1 mV |
| 13 | 0.0788 | 5.5 mV |
| **14** | **0.0572** | **4.0 mV** |

Under a hard max the floor is 0 and the margin goes with it. Simulated
directly (`--hardmax` swaps the diode-OR for an ideal `max()`):

| | xlse | xmax |
|---|---|---|
| floor = V_max − quiescent | 70.0 mV | **0.00 mV** |
| margin, worst channel | +4.0 mV | **+0.000 mV** |
| comparators at silence | all 0 | all at the metastable point |

At silence `max(v) = quiescent = V_ref`, so
`V_thr = alpha·V_ref + (1-alpha)·V_ref = V_ref` — the threshold lands exactly
on the signal **for every channel, whatever its alpha**. All 16 sit on their
trip point and a real comparator free-runs on noise.

We already knew soft max beat hard max by 6.7pp on accuracy. This is an
independent, circuit-level reason and it is the sharper one: a functional
failure rather than a score. It also means the LSE floor is not a side effect
of modelling the diode honestly — it is the thing that makes the shared-max
threshold work at all.

The flip side is that the worst channel has only 4.0 mV of it, and what eats
it is the per-channel quiescent scatter of §3. See §7 — that comparison is
close at typical V_OS and negative at the datasheet maximum, and it is the
binding constraint on the whole design.

## 3. The envelope quiescent is an amplified op-amp offset

Following the DC operating point by hand and confirming against the sim:

    V_env(quiescent) = V_da + (R5/R4)·(V_da - V_filt)

At quiescent D2 is reverse biased (dk = 750 mV, da = 895 mV — D1 clamps), so
C3 has no charging path. R5 is the only DC return, discharging C3 toward da.
The node settles where R5's current balances R4's, which happens (R5/R4) = 4.7
above da by whatever offset separates the filter output from the detector bias.

Simulated 918.38 mV; the colleague measures **917.83 mV**. 0.55 mV apart —
the sim tracks the board.

Consequences:

- **The envelope node is high impedance.** Anything hung on it moves it. The
  16 dividers alone pulled 0.9 µA and shifted an unbuffered reference 40 mV.
  Every reference must be buffered.
- **Each channel's quiescent is its own op-amp's offset × 4.7, random per
  device.** This is what killed the replica-detector idea (§4).

## 4. The replica detector does not work, and why

The plan had been a 17th detector with its input tied to the bias rail, so the
reference would drift with the real channels over supply and temperature.
Simulated, it lands 31 mV low, and the reason generalises:

- its R4 sits at `vmid`, while a real channel's sits at the filter output —
  which is itself two op-amp offsets below `vmid`. Fixing that needs a full
  replica *channel*, 3 more op-amps.
- and even then it cannot work, because the thing being tracked is each
  channel's *own* offset amplified 4.7×. That is per-device and random.
  Nothing passive tracks it.

So V_ref is a bring-up trim: a divider off the 1.8 V rail through a unity-gain
OPA379. In the netlist it is an ideal source, on purpose, so δ stays a single
sweepable knob matching training.

## 5. tau has a number now

CLAUDE.md §2.5 says the paper gives no value for the envelope time constant
and that ours is a figure-reading estimate of "a few ms". From the burst tail:

- **measured τ (1/e) = 3.16 ms**, half-life 2.24 ms
- nominal `R5·C3` = 4.70 ms; the OR diode discharges in parallel, which is why
  the measured value comes out lower

The burst also confirms the chain end to end: quiet at silence, comparator up
within ~1 ms of the tone starting and high for 99.2% of the burst, then down
and back to exactly the quiescent point after the tail.

This lands inside the estimated range, and it is ours rather than a guess at
someone else's. It does not change the baseline — CLAUDE.md §3.2 keeps
`envelope_tau_ms: 0` for stage 1 — but stage 2 now has a value to use instead
of an arbitrary one, which was the stated reason for not doing stage 2 yet.

## 6. Caveat on the absolute offsets

The OPA379 macromodel's offset is common-mode dependent:

| V_cm | output error |
|---|---|
| 0.5 V | +0.004 mV |
| 0.9 V (mid-rail) | −5.0 mV |

Mid-rail on a 1.8 V supply is the crossover region of a rail-to-rail input
stage, and macromodels handle it unevenly. **Whether the real part does this
has not been checked against the datasheet's Vos-vs-Vcm curve**, so treat the
absolute millivolts in §3 as model-dependent.

The mechanism is not. `(R5/R4)·offset` is topology: whatever offset the part
has, the detector multiplies it by 4.7. And the sim's own agreement with the
board's 917.83 mV suggests the model is not far wrong here.

One actionable hint if it holds up: biasing below mid-rail removed the offset
entirely in simulation. That trades away negative-half headroom, so it is a
real decision rather than a free win — flagged, not taken.

## 7. `head` — the constraint that binds

The silence margin (§2) and the quiescent scatter (§3) are the same design
pulling in two directions, and `sim_afe.table()` reports the ratio:

    margin_c = alpha_c · T·ln16                  grows with alpha only
    scatter  = (R5/R4) · V_OS + comparator_off   grows with detector gain
    head     = min(margin) / scatter

**Below 1×, the low-alpha channels sit below their own trip point at silence
and free-run on noise.** They stop carrying information — a dead channel, in
the sense CLAUDE.md §3.5 uses.

The asymmetry is the whole point. `T·ln16` is `n·V_T·ln16` — thermal, fixed at
~70 mV whatever the circuit does. The scatter is an op-amp offset multiplied
by the detector gain. **So detector gain buys signal and pays for it in
margin, one for one, and the floor never follows.**

### The numbers

OPA379 (SBOS347D): V_OS typ 0.4 mV, **max 1.5 mV**, 2 mV over −40..+125 °C.
Drift is 1.5 µV/°C, so this is a fixed per-device DC error, not something that
wanders at run time. Simulated:

| | R4 | R5 | C3 | preamp | gain | rise | τ | head @typ | head @max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original board | 10k | 47k | 100n | 10 | 4.70 | 247 mV | 3.13 ms | 1.39× | 0.50× |
| analog's current | 30k | 350k | 2.0n | 10 | 11.67 | 555 mV | 0.75 ms | **0.70×** | **0.21×** |
| **proposed** | 10k | 47k | 8.0n | **20** | 4.70 | 442 mV | 1.29 ms | **1.39×** | **0.50×** |

The proposed row is the finding: **same τ target and more signal than the
current values, at 2.8× less scatter**, because the level comes from the
preamp instead of the detector.

Preamp gain is free. 5 vs 10 vs 20 vs 30 changes `rise` and leaves quiescent,
floor, margin and scatter bit-for-bit identical — it sits ahead of the filter,
so its own offset never reaches the detector's summing node.

And R5 = 350 kΩ was never needed for a short τ. `τ = R5·C3`, so 47 kΩ with
8 nF gets there just as well, without the 2.5× gain that comes attached.

### What does not fix it

- **δ.** Restoring head at the current gain needs δ ≈ +15 mV, i.e. floor_frac
  ≈ 0.22. floor_frac 0.05 already killed four channels (test 0.732).
- **Per-channel α trim.** Absorbing the offset needs Δα = scatter/floor = 0.27
  at worst case. On ch14's α = 0.057 that is a 6× change — it kills the very
  channel it was meant to save.

Both work at the *typical* offset and neither survives the maximum, which is
the same statement as the table.

### Open

The bias rail sits at 0.9 V, and the datasheet specifies CMRR only for
V_CM ≤ (V+) − 1 V = **0.8 V on a 1.8 V supply**. We are above it. That is
consistent with §6's macromodel behaviour — ~0 offset at V_CM = 0.5 V, −5 mV
at mid-rail — which now looks less like a model artifact than it did.

Whether biasing lower is worth its headroom cost has not been worked out. It
is the one remaining lever that attacks the offset at the source rather than
dividing it down afterwards.
